# Severity-Case-Classification
### Pharmacovigilance Internship Project 
## 1. Introduction
Severity and case classification are important parts of pharmacovigilance. They help healthcare professionals understand the intensity of an adverse drug reaction (ADR) and determine how the case should be documented and reported.
Severity describes how strongly a reaction affects the patient, while case classification helps categorize the report according to specific criteria, such as seriousness and whether the reaction is expected or unexpected.
## 2. Objectives
- To understand the concepts of ADR severity and case classification.
- To differentiate between serious and non-serious adverse drug reactions.
- To understand expected and unexpected adverse reactions.
- To learn the basic steps involved in ADR case documentation.
- To improve knowledge of pharmacovigilance and patient safety.
## 3. Severity of an ADR
Severity refers to the intensity of an adverse reaction. It is commonly described as:
- **Mild:** The reaction causes minor discomfort and may not require specific treatment.
- **Moderate:** The reaction causes noticeable discomfort or may require medical treatment.
- **Severe:** The reaction is intense or significantly affects the patient's health and daily activities.
**Important:** Severe and serious do not mean the same thing. Severity describes the intensity of a reaction, whereas seriousness is determined by specific medical and regulatory criteria.
## 4. Case Classification
### A. Serious Case
An adverse reaction is considered serious if it results in any of the following:
- Death.
- A life-threatening condition.
- Hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- A congenital anomaly or birth defect.
- Another medically important event that may jeopardize the patient or require intervention to prevent one of these outcomes.
### B. Non-Serious Case
A non-serious case does not meet the established seriousness criteria. The reaction should still be documented and reported according to applicable guidelines.
### C. Expected Case
An expected adverse reaction is consistent with the applicable reference safety information for the medicine, such as the approved product information.
### D. Unexpected Case
An unexpected adverse reaction is not consistent with the applicable reference safety information, for example, because its nature, severity, or specificity differs.
### E. Individual Case
A report describing a suspected adverse reaction in an individual patient.
### F. Aggregate Case
A combined analysis of multiple cases or reports to identify patterns and potential drug safety signals. It is not the same as an individual case report.
## 5. Steps in Severity and Case Classification
1. **Collect information:** Record the patient's details, suspected medicine, reaction, and relevant medical history.
2. **Assess severity:** Evaluate the intensity of the reported reaction using the available clinical information.
3. **Assess seriousness:** Check whether the reaction meets any recognized seriousness criteria.
4. **Determine expectedness:** Compare the reaction with the applicable reference safety information.
5. **Classify the case:** Record the appropriate classifications based on the available evidence.
6. **Document and report:** Record the findings accurately and submit the report through the appropriate reporting system.
## 6. Examples
### Example 1: Serious Case
A patient develops a severe allergic reaction after taking a medicine and requires emergency hospitalization.
- Severity: Severe.
- Seriousness: Serious, because hospitalization occurred.
- Expectedness: Must be checked against the applicable reference safety information.
### Example 2: Non-Serious Case
A patient develops mild nausea after taking a medicine. The symptoms resolve without hospitalization or other serious outcomes.
- Severity: Mild.
- Seriousness: Non-serious, provided no seriousness criteria are met.
- Expectedness: Must be checked against the applicable reference safety information.
